Abstract

GANs are appealing because they generate sharp images with a single forward execution. Recent works have stabilized GAN training, but FID may still plateau on diverse datasets like ImageNet-256 even as training remains stable. We identify severe growth of discriminator activation magnitudes as a hidden pathology accompanying this plateau. Our analysis shows that adversarial training permits the discriminator to increase empirical real–fake separation by amplifying already-discriminative responses, without necessarily improving the underlying representation. Motivated by this observation, we redesign R3GAN’s network components to preserve activation magnitudes statistically. Across a roadmap of configurations, we perform diagnostic ablations showing that the gains depend primarily on the discriminator redesign. We train all generators and discriminators from scratch, without pretrained discriminator features. The resulting R3GAN2 models reach FIDs of 2.12 with 17 M parameters, 1.60 with 61 M, and 1.52 with 131 M on class-conditional ImageNet-256. At 1 NFE, this is substantially more efficient than competing diffusion and autoregressive methods, and than prior GAN baselines too (e.g., 10× fewer parameters than StyleGAN-XL for similar FID). We also train directly in pixel space, achieving FIDs of 1.65 on CIFAR-10 and 2.19 on FFHQ 256×256, where our generator achieves comparable FID to StyleGAN-XL with fewer than half as many parameters. In sum, identifying and removing this discriminator magnitude pathology yields a new SOTA in FID/parameter efficiency for GANs.
